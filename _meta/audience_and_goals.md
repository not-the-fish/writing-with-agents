# Audience and Goals

## Audiences by Content Type

### Newsletter

**Primary audience:** Data scientists and tech workers at or adjacent to the nonprofit/mission-driven sector. The ideal reader is someone who lives at the intersection — a data person working in a mission-driven context, or considering it. When the needs of a data-savvy reader and a nonprofit generalist conflict, the newsletter leans toward the data person.

**Secondary audience:** Nonprofit professionals who work with data teams or hire for data roles; tech workers exploring mission-driven career paths.

**What they come for:** Perspectives on the challenges of working with data, working on teams, staying agile, and self-development. The newsletter is primarily "here's how to think about this" — reframing problems, surfacing the right questions — rather than how-to. Readers should leave with a new way to frame something in their work.

### Creative Nonfiction

**Audience:** The literary essay audience — readers who are intensely curious about interiority, who read personal essays to understand how other minds work, and who want the combination of emotional honesty, intellectual rigor, and beautiful prose. People who read Febos, who subscribe to *The Sun* or *Guernica* or *Creative Nonfiction*, who browse essay collections at bookstores.

**What drives the writing:** An intense curiosity about other people's minds and lives, a dedication to self-reflection and self-improvement, and a desire to externalize a process of understanding — the world, other people, oneself. The goal is to offer readers that same experience: understanding and beautiful prose together.

### Business Development

**Audience:** Prospective clients and referral sources who encounter the marketing website or LinkedIn.

**Goal:** Hire or refer. The copy should make the writer's perspective and expertise obvious enough that the right people reach out.

**Positioning aspiration:** Opinionated but grounded. The core belief is that data work is deeply contextually embedded and outcome-driven — the goal is always to make things easier and clearer according to the specific needs of the organization. The current website copy is generic and lacks perspective; the older website had more of one. The new copy needs to close that gap.

### Gifts and Letters

**Audience:** Varies — each letter has a known audience of one.

**Aspiration:** Bring more of the creative nonfiction voice into letters. Be prompted toward more creativity, more of the vulnerability and figurative instincts from the personal essays, even in a casual or intimate register. This category is less about audience definition and more about craft development across registers.

## Definition of Success

### Newsletter
A piece is ready when it is useful and provides information or framing beyond what's available elsewhere. The bar is not just "accurate" but "worth someone's time over everything else they could read."

### Creative Nonfiction
A piece is ready when it is ready to submit for publication — meaning it meets the writer's own standards for structure, voice, and prose quality as defined by the rubrics, and is shaped for a specific outlet.
